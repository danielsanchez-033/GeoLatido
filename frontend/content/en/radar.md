# How radar sees

1. **It brings its own light.** NISAR does not take photos. It sends microwave pulses and measures the echo that comes back, so it sees the same by day or night, and through clouds.
2. **It measures shape and moisture, not color.** A rough or vegetated surface returns a strong echo and looks bright. Calm water and smooth ground bounce the echo away from the satellite and look dark. Wetter soil also changes the echo.
3. **It uses L-band, with waves about 24 cm long.** A wave that long partly passes through vegetation, so the echo mixes leaves, branches and ground.
4. **It listens in two ways: HH and HV.** HH is more sensitive to ground and water. HV grows when the signal bounces many times, as inside vegetation. Comparing both gives clues about what changed, not certainties.
5. **It only sees when it passes.** It flies over the same area, with the same geometry, every 12 days. Anything that happens and disappears between two passes is not recorded. That is why the answer is sometimes "not observable".
6. **What we do with it.** We compare two dates from the same series and mark where the echo changed more than a threshold. That says where the signal changed, not why. The cause is only offered as a hypothesis.

Sources: [NISAR mission, NASA JPL](https://nisar.jpl.nasa.gov/); 12-day repeat, [ISRO](https://www.isro.gov.in/NISARS_Band_SAR_Data_Products_Release.html).
